# The Oldest Trap in the World

**Logline:** Interest-bearing debt is older than coins, ancient kings kept having to cancel it, and the same math now runs through the credit card in your wallet.

**The loop:** A four thousand year old loan tablet, and a strange habit of Babylon's kings: every so often they declared loans void and had the tablets broken. Why would a king destroy his own kingdom's loans?

**The turn:** Debt is selling your future self's time at a discount.

**B08** · `TEXT` · music: history

## CH3 · The Clean Slate

**B16** · `TIMELINE` · music: history
VO: So the kings did something that sounds impossible today. More than four thousand years ago, in the Sumerian city of Lagash, a ruler named Urukagina declared debts forgiven. He freed people held in debt bondage and sent them home.
VISUAL: The timeline rolls back to "~2400 BC, Lagash". A small flat city wall rises. A ruler silhouette on the wall raises both hands and a line of freed candles walks out through the gate, blinking in the sunlight.
ON-SCREEN: ~2400 BC: Lagash, Urukagina

**B17** · `TEXT` · music: wonder
VO: The Sumerian word for this was amargi. It means return to the mother. It's often called one of the earliest written words for freedom. And the first time freedom was written down, it meant going home, free of debt.
VISUAL: The cuneiform sign for amargi draws on in amber at center, stroke by stroke. Below it, in marker: "amargi = return to the mother". A single freed candle in the distance walks toward a small house where a mother candle waits at the door with a lamp.
ON-SCREEN: amargi: "return to the mother"
SFX: chime

**B18** · `STAGE` · music: history
VO: The clearest example we have comes from Babylon, around sixteen forty six BC. King Ammisaduqa issued a decree called a misharum, which means justice or fairness. His is the only decree of its kind whose text survives almost complete.
VISUAL: Babylon's throne room again, grander. King Ammisaduqa silhouette on the throne. A herald candle unrolls a long list, far too long, that rolls down the steps and out the door. Background gag: a scribe candle in the corner is massaging his wrist.
ON-SCREEN: ~1646 BC: Ammisaduqa's edict

**B19** · `STACK` · music: warm

VISUAL: A tall wobbling stack of clay tablets labeled "family debts", "back taxes", "bondage contracts". A royal hammer taps the top and the whole stack cracks and crumbles into clay dust. Wick's son candle from B13 walks back up the road toward home. Wick, expression happy, runs to meet him.

**B20** · `SPLIT` · music: history
