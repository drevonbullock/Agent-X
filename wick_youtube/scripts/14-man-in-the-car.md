# Nobody Is Impressed by Your Car

**Logline:** By the end, the viewer understands Morgan Housel's "man in the car paradox", Thorstein Veblen's idea of conspicuous consumption, what surveys of real millionaires found about their cars, and why buying things to be admired almost never delivers the admiration.

**The loop:** A young valet in Los Angeles is handed the keys to a Ferrari and notices something strange about what goes through his own head. What did he think, and why does it matter for everything you buy to be seen?

**The turn:** People don't admire you in the car. They imagine themselves in it.

## CH2 · Put in Evidence

**B09** · `TIMELINE` · music: history
VO: In eighteen ninety nine, an American economist named Thorstein Veblen published a book called The Theory of the Leisure Class. It gave the world a phrase we still use. Conspicuous consumption.
VISUAL: A horizontal timeline scrolls backward from 2026 past 1950, 1920, and stops at 1899 with a thump. A flat printing press stamps out a book. Its cover reads "The Theory of the Leisure Class". Candle figures in top hats and long dresses stroll along the timeline behind it.
ON-SCREEN: 1899: "conspicuous consumption"
SFX: thud

**B10** · `TEXT` · music: history
VO: Conspicuous means easy to see. So conspicuous consumption means spending money mainly so other people can see that you spent it. Not for comfort. Not for use. For proof. Veblen was describing the rich of his day. It turned out to describe almost everyone.
VISUAL: Marker write on, word by word: "Spending" / "so others" / "can see." A large marker magnifying glass hovers over the word "see". Below, a flat top hat candle holds up a receipt to a crowd like a trophy.
ON-SCREEN: Spending so others can see.

**B11** · `QUOTE` · music: history
VO: Veblen wrote it like this. The wealth or power must be put in evidence, for esteem is awarded only on evidence. Esteem means respect. In other words, respect needs a receipt people can see.
VISUAL: Flat silhouette of a bearded man with a center part (generic Victorian profile). The quote writes on in marker: "The wealth or power must be put in evidence, for esteem is awarded only on evidence." Under it, smaller: "Thorstein Veblen, 1899." A rubber stamp thumps the word "EVIDENCE" in red ink at the end.
ON-SCREEN: Esteem is awarded only on evidence.
SFX: thud

**B12** · `CROWD` · music: history
VO: In Veblen's day, the evidence was things like fine clothes and household servants. Today it's a badge on a car, a watch, a bag with a logo. The props change. The play doesn't.
VISUAL: A crowd of small candles in 1899 outfits parade past with tall hats, canes, and a butler candle carrying a silver tray. A marker wipe transitions the same crowd to modern outfits: a flat watch glints, a handbag with a blank logo swings, a car badge shines. The candles walk the exact same path, step for step.
ON-SCREEN: The props change. The play doesn't.

**B13** · `CROWD` · music: history
VO: Veblen noticed one more thing. People mostly copy the spending of the group just above them. He called it emulation, which means trying to match someone you look up to. So the target never stops moving. There is always a car one step up.
VISUAL: A flat staircase of candles, each step a little fancier: a bicycle on the bottom step, a small car, a sedan, a convertible, a limousine at the top. Each candle is looking up at the step above, never down. At the very top, the limousine candle is looking up at a flat hot air balloon. Wick, on the second step, expression curious, cranes his neck.
ON-SCREEN: There's always a car one step up.

**B14** · `TEXT` · music: tension

VISUAL: Marker draws a dotted arrow labeled "evidence" flying from a car toward a candle labeled "respect". The arrow curves at the last second and misses, landing in a trash can. Wick, expression alarmed, watches it land.

## CH3 · The Mirror Problem

**B15** · `STAGE` · music: tension
VO: Let's run the experiment with Wick. He buys a car he can barely afford. Glossy paint, a badge on the hood. And he drives it, slowly, down a street full of people.
VISUAL: Wick behind the wheel of a gleaming flat convertible, expression proud, chin up. He rolls slowly down a long flat street lined with candle figures on both sidewalks. Background gag: a small dog on the sidewalk is the only one looking at Wick himself, and it's unimpressed.
ON-SCREEN: The experiment
SFX: soft engine hum

**B16** · `CROWD` · music: tension
VO: Every person on that street sees the car. And every one of them, for just a second, imagines themselves inside it. Nobody on that street is thinking about Wick. The car is the star of a movie playing in every head on that street, and Wick isn't cast in any of them.
VISUAL: Camera tracks along the sidewalk. Above each candle's head, a thought bubble pops up in sequence, pop pop pop. Every bubble shows that candle in the driver's seat: one waving, one in sunglasses, one with a tiny crown. Not a single bubble shows Wick. Wick, driving, expression happy, has no idea.
ON-SCREEN: Nobody is thinking about Wick.
SFX: pop, pop, pop

**B17** · `ZOOM` · music: tension
VO: That's the paradox in full. People want wealth to signal that they deserve admiration. But others often skip admiring you. As Housel puts it, they use your wealth as a benchmark, a measuring stick, for their own desire to be liked and admired.
VISUAL: Zoom in on one sidewalk candle's thought bubble. Inside it, a flat measuring stick appears next to the car, and the candle measures itself against it, standing on tiptoe. Zoom back out: every candle on the street is holding its own little measuring stick.
ON-SCREEN: Your car is their measuring stick.

**B18** · `STAGE` · music: tension

## THE TURN

**B27** · `TEXT` · music: wonder
VO: Here it is. People don't admire you in the car. They imagine themselves in it. The admiration you're paying for is real. It's just pointed at the car, and every stranger who walks by gets to borrow it for free.
VISUAL: Big marker write on, two lines: "They don't admire you." then "They imagine themselves." The second line glows amber. Wick stands small at the bottom of the frame, looking up at the words, expression calm.
ON-SCREEN: They imagine themselves in it.
SFX: chime

**B28** · `ZOOM` · music: wonder
VO: Once you see that, the car stops being a question about other people. It becomes a question about you. Do you love driving it? Wonderful. That's a real reason. Is it a message to strangers? Then know it will never be delivered.
VISUAL: Zoom out from Wick's car on the street, up over the town, until every car on every road is a tiny dot, each trailing a small undelivered envelope that floats off into the sky like a lost balloon. Zoom back in to Wick in the driver's seat, expression focused, deciding.
ON-SCREEN: Who is it for?

**B29** · `QUOTE` · music: wonder

VISUAL: The generic profile silhouette from B08 returns. The line writes on in marker: "Humility, kindness, and empathy will bring you more respect than horsepower ever will." The word "horsepower" is drawn with a tiny cartoon horse galloping off the edge of the page.
ON-SCREEN: More respect than horsepower ever will.

**B30** · `STAGE` · music: warm
VO: Think about the people you actually respect. You remember how they treated you. You probably can't name what they drove. That kind of respect is free, and it never needs a loan.
VISUAL: Wick sits on a park bench, expression happy, flipping through a small photo album. Each photo shows a different candle doing a kindness: holding a door, sharing an umbrella, teaching Wick to ride a bike. In the corner of each photo, a car is drawn as a blank grey outline, forgotten.
ON-SCREEN: Respect is free.

## PAYOFF

**B31** · `STAGE` · music: resolve
VO: Back to that hotel driveway. The Ferrari pulls away. Its driver thinks the whole street is watching. And the street is watching. It just isn't watching him. Every person on it is sitting in that seat, in their head. That's what the valet saw.
VISUAL: Return to the B01 hotel driveway at golden hour. The red car pulls away. Every candle on the sidewalk has a thought bubble with themselves in the seat. Wick in the red vest, expression calm, holds the empty key hook and quietly smiles, because he finally understands. The pigeon from B01 has a thought bubble too, showing itself driving.
ON-SCREEN: That's what the valet saw.

**B32** · `CROWD` · music: resolve
VO: Here's the gentle twist. You're on that street too. When you buy something to be seen, you're the driver. When you watch someone else's, you're the crowd. Almost all of us play both parts.
VISUAL: The camera pans across the crowd and finds Wick twice: once on the sidewalk with a thought bubble, and once behind the wheel of a passing car. The two Wicks spot each other and wave, expression curious on both.
ON-SCREEN: You play both parts.

**B33** · `SPLIT` · music: resolve
VO: So imagine two versions of Wick. One sends that payment every month for seven years. The other buys a car he likes that costs three hundred dollars a month less, and keeps the difference. That's twenty five thousand two hundred dollars over seven years, before it earns a cent.
VISUAL: Left: Wick, expression weary, feeding envelopes into a mailbox shaped like a car, 84 times in a fast loop. Right: Wick, expression determined, in a plain car, dropping coins into a jar that fills as a counter above it rolls to $25,200. A corner tag reads "Illustrative: $300 x 84 months".
