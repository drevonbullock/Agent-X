# How Rome Destroyed Its Own Money

**Logline:** How Rome's silver coin went from almost pure silver to a copper coin in a silver coat, why emperors did it, why the emperor's price freeze failed, and how the same slow mechanism touches the cash in your account today.

**The loop:** How do you rob an entire empire without stealing a single coin?

**The turn:** Money is a promise. Inflation is the promise being diluted.

## CH1 · A Coin You Trust

**B04** · `TIMELINE` · music: history
VO: Rewind about three centuries. Augustus, Rome's first emperor, rules an empire that runs on one coin. The denarius, Rome's everyday silver coin. It weighs about three point nine grams, and it is almost pure silver.
VISUAL: A parchment timeline scrolls right to left from 268 back to 27 BC. A flat Augustus silhouette in profile rises at the start. A single denarius spins into the center, big and shiny, then settles onto a tiny balance scale opposite a small 3.9 g weight; the scale sits level.
ON-SCREEN: Denarius: ~3.9 g, nearly pure silver
SFX: tick, chime

**B05** · `STAGE` · music: history
VO: That purity mattered. When a baker in Rome took a denarius, he wasn't trusting the emperor's face. He was trusting the metal. If Rome fell tomorrow, the silver would still be silver. The stamp was a promise, and the metal kept it.
VISUAL: A Roman market street: bread stall, awnings, stacked amphorae. Wick in a plain tunic hands a coin to a baker candle, who weighs it on a hand scale, nods, and hands over a round loaf. Wick is happy. Background gag: a tiny pigeon pecks crumbs off the scale, and the scale dips slightly.
ON-SCREEN: The stamp promised. The metal proved.

**B06** · `MACHINE` · music: wonder
VO: Think of a coin as two things fused together. A promise, stamped on the outside: this is worth one denarius. And proof, inside: enough silver to back the promise up. As long as the two match, the coin works. People trust it without thinking.
VISUAL: A flat mechanical press. A blank silver disc slides in on a conveyor. The top die slams down with the word PROMISE; the bottom plate glows with the word PROOF. The finished coin rolls out with two gears on it turning in sync. A green check mark pops above.
ON-SCREEN: Coin = promise + proof

**B07** · `COUNTER` · music: tension

VISUAL: A big number counter labeled "Silver in a denarius" rolls down from 98% to about 80%. Beside it, a flat Nero silhouette holds a lyre, looking pleased. The denarius beside the counter shrinks a hair and a small wedge of it turns copper orange.
ON-SCREEN: 64 AD: about 80% silver

**B08** · `STAGE` · music: tension

## CH2 · The Soldier Problem

**B09** · `CROWD` · music: tension
VO: Because every emperor had one bill he could never skip. The army. Rome's emperors were made by soldiers, and sometimes unmade by them. A soldier who went unpaid did not write a complaint. He found a new emperor.

**B10** · `CHART_BAR` · music: tension

**B13** · `FLOW` · music: tension

VISUAL: Split screen. Left: two old denarii side by side, labeled "2". Right: one new, slightly larger coin with the emperor wearing a spiky radiate crown, labeled "worth 2". A scale under each; the right scale only reaches the "1.5" tick. A tiny coin slides from the gap into a palace purse.

## CH3 · The Great Thinning

**B16** · `CHART_LINE` · music: tension
VO: Over the next half century, emperor after emperor took another slice. By the early two hundred and fifties, the double coin was only about one fifth silver. By the late two hundred and sixties, it was under five percent. Some coins held about two and a half.
VISUAL: A line chart labeled "Silver in Rome's main coin" draws itself left to right from 215 to 270. It starts high, stair steps down, then plunges near the end. Tiny coin icons sit at each step, each one a little more orange. The final dot lands at 2.5% with a small thud.
ON-SCREEN: 20% in 250s. Under 5% by 268.
SFX: thud

**B17** · `STAGE` · music: tension
VO: Here is what that feels like on the ground. You are the baker again. People keep paying you in coins that hold less and less silver. You are not a fool. So you ask for more coins for the same loaf.
VISUAL: The bread stall. The baker candle looks at a pile of pale orange coins, then at the loaf, then crosses out a price sign and writes a higher one. Wick, as a customer in a toga, digs in his purse, expression weary. Background gag: the baker's sign has been crossed out so many times it is now three stacked boards.
ON-SCREEN: Same loaf. More coins.

**B18** · `TEXT` · music: wonder
VO: That is inflation in its oldest form. Inflation just means prices rising, because each unit of money is worth less. The bread did not become more valuable. The coin became less of a coin.
VISUAL: Marker write on, big: "Inflation". Underneath, a definition writes on in smaller hand. A loaf on the left stays exactly the same size. A coin on the right shrinks with each word.
ON-SCREEN: Inflation: each coin buys less

**B19** · `STAGE` · music: tension
VO: So the emperors now had a new problem, and it was their own fault. Everything cost more, including the army. More coins had to be struck to pay for it. Each round of thinning demanded the next. What does a strong ruler do when prices won't behave? He orders them to stop.
VISUAL: Palace interior. A flat mint machine churns out coins faster and faster; each coin is more orange than the last. A sign on the mint door: "NOW HIRING. NO SILVER EXPERIENCE NEEDED." Wick stands beside the machine in a toga holding a clipboard, expression sombre.
ON-SCREEN: Thin the coin. Pay more. Repeat.
SFX: tick, tick, tick

## CH4 · The Emperor's Price List

**B20** · `STAGE` · music: history
VO: In the year three hundred and one, the emperor Diocletian did exactly that. He issued the Edict on Maximum Prices. It was a giant price list, carved in stone and set up in cities across the empire's eastern half, where most surviving copies were found. More than a thousand goods and services, each with a legal ceiling.
VISUAL: A flat stone tablet slides up out of the ground in a town square, taller than the buildings. Lines of tiny text scroll down its face. Diocletian's silhouette stands proudly beside it. Wick, among a crowd in togas, stares up at it, expression curious.
ON-SCREEN: 301 AD: Edict on Maximum Prices
SFX: thud

**B21** · `COUNTER` · music: history
VO: A pound of pork, twelve denarii. Wages for laborers and craftsmen, all capped. And the punishment for charging more? Death. The edict said the same for anyone who hid goods to avoid selling them.
VISUAL: A counter card shows "Pork: 12 denarii / lb" with a flat pig icon. Below it, wage cards flip over quickly. The last card flips to a flat skull and crossed swords symbol. The pig icon's eyes widen (background gag).
ON-SCREEN: Break the price? Death.

**B22** · `QUOTE` · music: tension
VO: The edict even explained why. In its opening words, the emperors complained that a soldier could lose his bonus and his whole salary in a single purchase. The very army the coins were thinned to pay was now being crushed by the prices.
VISUAL: A parchment scroll unrolls. A flat soldier silhouette holds out a full purse, hands it to a merchant, and walks away holding an empty one. The quote writes on in marker.
ON-SCREEN: "A soldier is deprived of his bonus"

**B23** · `QUOTE` · music: tension
VO: It did not go well. A Christian writer named Lactantius, who lived through it, described what happened. Much blood was shed over trifles. Men were afraid to put anything up for sale. The scarcity got worse, until the law was finally dropped.
VISUAL: Flat silhouette of Lactantius writing by lamplight. Behind him, the market street: stalls with their shutters slamming closed one by one. Shelves empty. Wick stands in the empty street with a basket, expression alarmed.
ON-SCREEN: "Afraid to expose anything to sale"
SFX: thud, thud, thud

**B24** · `SPLIT` · music: history
VO: An honest footnote. Lactantius hated Diocletian, who persecuted Christians, so he may have painted it darker than it was. But whatever the exact story, the price list did not hold. Because the prices were never the real problem.
VISUAL: Split. Left: Lactantius with a small storm cloud over his head, labeled "Not a fan." Right: the stone price tablet with a crack running down it, weeds growing at its base. Wick between the two, expression calm, hands raised in a gentle "fair enough" shrug.
ON-SCREEN: Biased witness. Same result.

## THE TURN

**B25** · `TEXT` · music: wonder

**B26** · `MACHINE` · music: wonder
VO: Diluting is quiet. It is like adding water to wine. Nobody sees the cup get emptier. It just tastes a little thinner each time. Rome never took a single coin back from anyone. It just made every coin mean less. And a price list cannot fix a promise. Only the thing behind the promise can.
VISUAL: Return to the coin press from B06. Now the PROMISE die still slams down with the same stamp, but the PROOF plate underneath dims to a faint glow. The two gears slip out of sync and grind. The green check mark from B06 flickers and turns into a question mark.
ON-SCREEN: Same stamp. Less behind it.

## CH5 · Your Dollar Today

**B27** · `ZOOM` · music: wonder
VO: Now fast forward about seventeen centuries. The dollar in your pocket holds no silver at all. It is a promise backed by trust in the economy, and by the Federal Reserve, America's central bank, which manages how much money exists. Inflation today works the same basic way. More money chasing the same amount of stuff.
VISUAL: Zoom out from the Roman coin through time: parchment tones fade to the PAPER palette. The coin becomes a flat modern dollar bill, then a phone showing a bank app balance. Wick in a hoodie holds the phone, expression focused.
ON-SCREEN: More money, same stuff
SFX: whoosh

**B28** · `COUNTER` · music: warm
VO: The Bureau of Labor Statistics tracks it with the consumer price index. That is a big basket of things people buy, priced every month. By its numbers, a hundred dollars of shopping in the year two thousand costs about one hundred and ninety four dollars today.
VISUAL: A flat shopping cart fills with icons: bread, gas can, rent key, a movie ticket. A price tag counter above the cart rolls from $100 up to $194. A small calendar flips from 2000 to 2026.
ON-SCREEN: $100 in 2000 = $194 today
SFX: tick

**B29** · `STACK` · music: warm
VO: Flip that around. A dollar you hid under your mattress in two thousand now buys about half of what it used to. Over the twelve months to August two thousand twenty six, prices rose three point four percent.
VISUAL: A flat mattress. Wick lifts the corner, expression weary, and pulls out a stack of bills. The stack is the same height, but each bill fades to half opacity. A small tag reads "still says $1."
ON-SCREEN: Aug 2026 inflation: 3.4% a year

**B30** · `SPLIT` · music: warm
VO: And to be clear, this is not Rome. Modern inflation is measured and published every month. The Federal Reserve even aims for about two percent a year, on purpose, as a sign of a healthy economy. Nobody is secretly biting your coins. But the arithmetic is the same.
VISUAL: Split. Left: a Roman mint in chaos, coins flying. Right: a tidy flat office with a chart on the wall labeled "Target: 2%", a calm candle at a desk. Wick walks from left to right, expression calm, and sets a tiny helmet down on the desk.
ON-SCREEN: Not collapse. Just arithmetic.

## PAYOFF

**B31** · `STAGE` · music: resolve
VO: So how do you rob an empire without stealing a coin? You leave every coin exactly where it is, and you make each one mean a little less. Money sitting in an account that pays less than inflation is doing the same thing to you. Slowly. The number stays the same. What it can buy gets smaller.
VISUAL: Callback to B01. The army camp at dusk, same composition. Wick the soldier holds the bitten coin up to the light, then it morphs into a phone showing a savings balance: the digits stay the same while a small bread loaf icon beside them shrinks frame by frame. Expression determined.
ON-SCREEN: Same number. Buys less.
