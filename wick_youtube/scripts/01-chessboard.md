# The Chessboard That Bankrupted a King

**Logline:** Through an old legend about a king, a chessboard and a few grains of wheat, you'll see why compounding (earning returns on your returns) looks like nothing for years and then outruns everything, and why the year you start matters more than how much you put in.

**The loop:** How can ten years of doing a little beat thirty years of doing a lot?

**The turn:** The first squares look like nothing, and that is exactly why people quit. Time is the one ingredient you can't buy later.

4. World wheat production a little over 800 million tonnes a year (USDA puts 2024/25 at about 799 million and the record 2025/26 crop at about 840 million). Math: 738 to 1,199 billion tonnes ÷ ~844 million tonnes a year = about 875 to 1,420 years; script rounds to "roughly a thousand years" — [USDA WASDE, May 2026 (PDF)](https://www.usda.gov/oce/commodity/wasde/wasde0526v2.pdf); [USDA ERS, Wheat Outlook: March 2026 (PDF)](https://ers.usda.gov/sites/default/files/_laserfiche/outlooks/113938/WHS-26c.pdf?v=80852)
5. US stock market (S&P 500 with dividends) compound annual return of about 10% from 1928 through 2025, about 7% after inflation; single year losses of more than a third (for example 1931 and 2008) — [Aswath Damodaran, NYU Stern, "Historical Returns on Stocks, Bonds and Bills"](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html); [Damodaran, "Data Update 2 for 2025"](https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/data-update-2-for-2025-the-party)
6. Illustrative compounding math (the channel's own, reproducible): $200 a month, 7% a year after inflation, compounded monthly at 1.07^(1/12) − 1. Future value of monthly deposits = 200 × ((1+r)^n − 1) / r.
   - Year 1: $2,400 in, $2,476 total, $76 growth.
   - Year 10: $24,000 in, $34,210 total, $10,210 growth.
   - Age 25 to 65 (480 months): $96,000 in, $494,308 total ($398,308 growth, about 81%).
   - Age 35 to 65 (360 months): $72,000 in, $233,891 total.
   - Age 25 to 35 only (120 months), then left alone 30 years: $34,210 × 1.07^30 = $260,418 on $24,000 in.
   - The $260,418 equals the early starter's last ten years of growth ($494,308 − $233,891), which is why B30 says the late starter "lost the last ten".
