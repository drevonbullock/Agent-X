# Why Poor People Stay Poor (It's Not Laziness)

**ABT:** Everyone needs boots, AND people without money can only afford cheap ones, BUT cheap boots wear out
and have to be bought again and again, THEREFORE being poor costs more, and the way out is a small cushion,
not more willpower.

**Logline:** Two Wicks with the same job and the same pay. One stays broke no matter how hard he works. Through
Terry Pratchett's boots theory and real numbers, you'll see why being poor costs more, why it's a trap and not
laziness, and the ten dollar move that breaks it.

**Open loops:** Q1 (0:15) "Over ten years, who spends more on boots?" → paid at ~1:25. Q2 (0:30) "The way out costs
less than one pair of cheap boots" → paid at ~6:05. Setup (0:10): Dee's little jar labelled BOOTS on her locker
shelf → payoff at ~5:45.

**Upgrades over N01 (Dre: "improve them as we go"):**
1. Hook in 10 s, mid-action (N01 took 39 s to reach its question).
2. A running SCOREBOARD (Wick vs Dee, live cost counters) threads the whole episode instead of one-off builds.
3. Viewer predicts, then the reveal (Q1), and two open loops paid off later.
4. Two music moods: tense night-city bed until the turn, then hopeful. A 1 s silence before "It's not laziness."
5. A signature sound: the wet squelch of the cheap boot, which goes silent when he finally buys good ones.

**Casting (Wick plays every role, flame tip always showing):** Wick, night security guard (navy guard cap,
flashlight, cheap boots with a split sole). Dee, his coworker (same cap, a red scarf), same job, same pay.
Farmer Wicks (straw hats) in CH3. A bank-teller Wick behind glass, a payday-lender Wick with a neon sign.

**Palettes:** navy = the rainy night story, green = how money works (the scoreboard, the loop), mustard = the book
and the India study. The SCOREBOARD is a green chalk-board strip that docks at the top of the frame whenever money moves.

**Music:** `tense` (0:00–4:40): low plucked strings, soft rain texture, a ticking pulse. `hope` (4:40–end):
warm piano and strings, same key. Silence for ~1 s before B19's key line.

## CH1 · The Boots Theory

**B05** · `BOOK` · music: tense
VO: The idea comes from a novel. In nineteen ninety three, the writer Terry Pratchett gave one of his characters, a city guard, a theory about boots. That guard earned thirty eight dollars a month.
VISUAL: Mustard paper. An old hardback falls open; ink rain drips off its pages. A guard's silhouette walks out
of the page onto a cobbled street. A pay slip flutters down: $38 / month.
ON-SCREEN: Terry Pratchett · Men at Arms · 1993

**B06** · `SCOREBOARD` · music: tense
VO: Good leather boots cost fifty dollars. More than a whole month's pay. But they last about ten years. Cheap boots cost ten dollars. But they only last a season or two.
VISUAL: Back to the scoreboard. The $50 tag towers over the $38 pay slip. Dee's lane: one pair, a bar that
stretches the full ten years. Wick's lane: one pair, a bar that stops at year one and a half, then cracks.

**B07** · `SCOREBOARD` · music: tense
VO: So when his boots fall apart, the guard can't find fifty dollars at once. Therefore he buys cheap ones again. And again. And again.
VISUAL: Wick's lane fills with pairs, pop, pop, pop, each one cracking as the next arrives; the dead pairs pile up
behind him like a little mountain. His counter ticks: $10, $20, $30… Dee's counter sits at $50.
SFX: pop + cash-register tick per pair

**B08** · `SCOREBOARD` · music: tense
VO: After ten years, he's spent a hundred dollars on boots. Twice what the rich guard paid. And his feet are still wet.
VISUAL: Counters lock: WICK $100, DEE $50. The "?" from B03 flips to "WICK ×2". A single drip lands on Wick's
counter. (Q1 paid, ~1:25.)
ON-SCREEN: 10 years: $100 vs $50

**B09** · `SCOREBOARD` · music: tense
VO: Here's the key. The poor guard isn't bad with money. The good boots aren't too expensive. They're too expensive all at once. That's the whole trap, and once you see it, you see it everywhere.
VISUAL: The $50 tag splits into five $10 coins, then snaps back into one heavy $50 block that Wick can't lift.
The words ALL AT ONCE stamp under it.
ON-SCREEN: Not too expensive. Too expensive ALL AT ONCE.

**B10** · `SCOREBOARD` · music: tense
VO: In real life, you pay this boots tax everywhere. Let's follow Wick and add up what being broke costs him.
VISUAL: The scoreboard relabels itself: BROKE TAX. Wick's counter resets to $0 and a calendar strip appears
under it. Dee's counter reads $0 too, and stays there all chapter.

## CH2 · The Broke Tax

**B11** · `BANK` · music: tense

VISUAL: A phone screen: balance $1. The coffee cup slides across; the balance goes red, −$3. A bank-teller Wick

ON-SCREEN: Typical overdraft fee: $35

**B12** · `BANK` · music: tense

VISUAL: A mountain of $35 tags builds into a "$12 BILLION" pile. Then a crowd of 100 tiny ink figures; 9 of them

ON-SCREEN: ~80% of fees paid by fewer than 9% of accounts

**B13** · `BANK` · music: tense

**B14** · `PAYDAY` · music: tense
VO: In March, the car breaks down. He needs three hundred dollars by Friday, or he can't get to work. A payday lender says yes. The fee is fifteen dollars for every hundred. That sounds small. Over a year, it works out to almost four hundred percent interest.
VISUAL: The car coughs smoke. A payday-lender Wick under a buzzing neon sign slides $300 across. A "$45 due in 2
weeks" tag. Then a percentage meter spins up past 20%, 100%, 200%… and stops at ~400%. Counter: $80.
ON-SCREEN: $15 per $100 ≈ 400% a year

**B15** · `PAYDAY` · music: tense
VO: And most people can't pay it back in two weeks. So they borrow again to cover it. More than four out of five of these loans get rolled over or renewed. The fee keeps coming back.
VISUAL: The $45 tag spawns another loan, which spawns another: a chain of tags linking across the frame. Five
little loan icons; four of them loop back into new loans.
ON-SCREEN: 4 in 5 rolled over or renewed

**B16** · `STORE` · music: tense
VO: At the store, the big pack of toilet paper is cheaper per roll. But it costs more today. So Wick buys the small one. Families with less money could save around eight percent a roll just by buying bigger packs. If they had the cash today.
VISUAL: A shelf: a 4-pack and a 24-pack with per-roll price tags. Wick's wallet opens: not enough for the big
one. He takes the small pack; a "+8%" sticker slaps onto it.

**B17** · `CARLOT` · music: tense
VO: Then a late payment hits his credit score. And a lower score makes everything cost more. Take the same thirty thousand dollar car. With great credit, the loan costs about three and a half thousand dollars in interest. With bad credit, more than eleven thousand.
VISUAL: Two identical cars side by side. Dee's price tag: interest $3,480. Wick's tag grows: interest $11,440.
The gap between them fills with stacked bills.
ON-SCREEN: 4.4% vs 13.5% APR · 5-year loan

**B18** · `SCOREBOARD` · music: tense → hit
VO: Same car. Same job. Almost eight thousand dollars more. Add it all up, and being broke has a price. Dee pays none of it. Not because she works harder. Because she can afford the cheaper way.
VISUAL: The calendar strip fills; Wick's counter races up and slams past $8,000. Dee's reads $0. Big hit, the
music drops out for a beat, then the tense bed returns lower. (Reset 1.)
ON-SCREEN: BROKE TAX · WICK $8,000+ · DEE $0

## CH3 · The Tired Brain

**B19** · `STREET` · music: tense (lower)
VO: So why doesn't Wick just plan better? Why not budget, shop around, read the fine print? Here's the part most people miss.
VISUAL: Back on the rainy street. Wick stares at a stack of bills on the guard-hut desk, each one a little
brighter than the last, all shouting for attention.

**B20** · `MALL` · music: tense (curious)
VO: In one well known study, researchers went to a shopping mall in New Jersey. They asked people to imagine their car needed a repair. For some, the repair cost one hundred fifty dollars. For others, fifteen hundred. Then everyone took a thinking test.
VISUAL: Mustard paper. A mall atrium; shopper Wicks at a little table. A thought bubble: a car with a $150 tag,
then a car with a $1,500 tag. Puzzle cards slide in front of them.

**B21** · `MALL` · music: tense
VO: With the cheap repair, richer and poorer people scored about the same. With the expensive one, the poorer people's scores dropped. Just thinking about a money problem they couldn't solve made it harder to think.
VISUAL: Two pairs of score bars. $150: RICHER and POORER level. $1,500: POORER bar sinks while RICHER stays.

**B22** · `INDIA` · music: tense
VO: Then the same team went to India and tested sugarcane farmers twice. Once right before harvest, when money was tight. And once right after, when they had just been paid. Same farmers. Same brains.
VISUAL: Mustard map of India; a pin drops on Tamil Nadu. Zoom into green cane fields. Farmer Wicks in straw hats
at a table with puzzle cards. A calendar flips from BEFORE HARVEST to AFTER HARVEST.
ON-SCREEN: 464 farmers · 54 villages

**B23** · `INDIA` · music: tense
VO: Before harvest, they did worse. After they got paid, they did better. The researchers compared the gap to losing a full night of sleep. Scientists still argue about how big this effect really is. But anyone who has been broke knows the feeling.
VISUAL: Two score bars: BEFORE 4.35, AFTER 5.45 correct. A little moon-and-pillow icon slides in between them.

**B24** · `BRAIN` · music: tense
VO: They call it a bandwidth tax. When money is short, worry takes up room in your head. You fight today's emergency, and next month's problem slips out of view. Wick isn't ignoring the fine print. He's just out of room.
VISUAL: Inside Wick's head: a bandwidth bar. Bills pour in and fill it to red. Then a tunnel: his view narrows to a
single glowing bill while next month's calendar and the fine print fade into the dark at the edges.

**B25** · `STREET` · music: silence ~1 s, then tense
VO: It's not laziness. Six million Americans spent most of the year working or looking for work, and still ended up below the poverty line. It's a trap.
VISUAL: Silence. Wick, lit by his own flame, walking his shift in the rain. Then the city pulls back: thousands
of tiny lit windows, guard-cap icons filling the skyline. (Reset 2.)
ON-SCREEN: 6.1 million working poor (BLS, 2023)

## THE TURN

**B26** · `LOOP` · music: tense → hope (builds)
VO: Here's how the trap works. No savings. Then something breaks. Therefore you pay the expensive way: a fee, a loan, the small pack. That leaves you with less. Therefore, still no savings. And round it goes.
VISUAL: Green board. A circle draws itself in four stations: NO CUSHION → SOMETHING BREAKS → PAY THE EXPENSIVE WAY
→ LESS MONEY → back to NO CUSHION. Wick runs around it like a hamster wheel, faster each lap.

**B27** · `LOOP` · music: hope (enters)
VO: It's not rare. More than a third of American adults couldn't cover a four hundred dollar emergency with cash. For them, every surprise gets the expensive fix, and the loop spins again.
VISUAL: A $400 repair bill drops into the loop; a grid of 100 adult icons, 37 of them tinted grey.
ON-SCREEN: 37% can't cover $400 in cash (Fed, 2025)

**B28** · `LOOP` · music: hope
VO: But look at the loop one more time. It only keeps spinning because of one link. No cushion. Break that one link, and the whole thing slows down.
VISUAL: The camera pushes in on the NO CUSHION station; it glows red. Wick's hands snip it. The circle stops.
Wick stumbles off the wheel, blinking.

## PAYOFF

**B29** · `LOCKER` · music: hope
VO: Remember Dee? She isn't smarter than Wick. She doesn't work harder. She just had fifty dollars saved in a little jar when her boots wore out.
VISUAL: Back in the guard hut: the BOOTS jar on Dee's shelf from B02, now in focus. She tips out fifty dollars and
buys the good boots. (Setup paid.)

**B30** · `SCOREBOARD` · music: hope
VO: And it shows up in the numbers. A government survey found people with no emergency savings were almost nine times as likely to overdraft as people with plenty saved. Another study found people with a cushion spent about half as much time each week worrying about money.
VISUAL: Two jars: empty vs full. Above them, overdraft bars: 35% vs 4%. Then two clocks: 7.3 hrs vs 3.7 hrs of
worry a week; Wick's bandwidth bar drains from red to calm.
ON-SCREEN: Overdrafted: 35% vs 4% · Worry: 7.3 vs 3.7 hrs/week

**B31** · `LOCKER` · music: hope
VO: So here's the way out, and it costs less than one pair of cheap boots. Ten dollars. Every week. That's five hundred and twenty dollars a year. Enough to cover that four hundred dollar emergency, with money left over.
VISUAL: Wick puts a $10 bill in his own jar; a 52-week grid fills one square per week, the jar rising, a $400 line
crossed at week 40. (Q2 paid.)
ON-SCREEN: $10 × 52 = $520

**B32** · `CARLOT` · music: hope
VO: The next time something breaks, Wick pays with cash. No fee. No loan. Just a receipt. And for the first time, the loop stops. His head gets a little quieter. And he can finally plan.
VISUAL: The car coughs again. Wick pays from the jar. The BROKE TAX counter stops ticking, then flips to $0. The
loop icon in the corner shatters into sparkles. His bandwidth bar shows free space.

## CLOSE

**B33** · `STREET` · music: hope
VO: So here's your first step. Tonight, open a separate savings account and name it boots. Set ten dollars to move in every week. If that's too much, start with five. A high yield account pays around four percent right now, so it even grows.
VISUAL: A phone screen, Wick's thumb: NEW ACCOUNT → name field types "BOOTS" → AUTO-TRANSFER $10 / WEEK → a
toggle clicks ON. The jar icon appears on his home screen.
ON-SCREEN: Separate account · named BOOTS · $10 every week

**B34** · `STREET` · music: hope
VO: And the next time you buy something cheap, ask one question. Will I be buying this again next year? If the answer is yes, that's your boots.
VISUAL: A quick montage of cheap things on a shelf (a phone charger, a frying pan, sneakers), each flipping a tag
that reads AGAIN NEXT YEAR?

**B35** · `STREET` · music: hope (final swell, then stop)

VISUAL: Dawn over the city. Wick, in new leather boots, steps into the same puddle from B01. No squelch. He looks
down, then grins. Dee bumps his shoulder. Subscribe lower-third pops in; hold, fade.
SFX: the squelch is silent
