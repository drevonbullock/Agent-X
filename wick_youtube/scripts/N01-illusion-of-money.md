# The Illusion of Money

**Logline:** Through the true story of Yap's giant stone money, you'll see that money is a story we all agree to believe, why that story slowly loses its power, and why the one thing money can never make more of is your time.

**The loop:** How can a family be rich from a fortune nobody could see, touch or reach?

**The turn:** Money is a story. Your time is real. So measure every price in hours of your life, not dollars.

**Casting (Wick plays every role, in costume):** Islander Wicks (flower leis), a Chief Wick (feathered headband), German Official Wick (pith helmet), Captain O'Keefe Wick (captain's cap), Wick A the show-off (gold chain) and Wick B the free one (plain). The face, flame and wax never change, and the flame tip always shows.

**Palettes:** navy = the story, green = how money works, mustard = history, and deep teal for underwater (from the approved montage).

## CH3 · The Black Crosses

**B14** · `STAGE` · music: history

## THE TURN

**B24** · `MONTAGE` · music: cosmic

**B25** · `STAGE` · music: cosmic

## PAYOFF

**B29** · `COUNTER` · music: warm

ON-SCREEN: $1,000 = 50 hours of your life

**B30** · `SPLIT` · music: warm

ON-SCREEN: Real wealth = months of freedom

**B32** · `SPLIT` · music: resolve

1. The sunken stone ("lying at the bottom of the sea for two or three generations" after an ancestor's raft was caught in a storm; the family's wealth was still recognised), the black crosses painted on stones by German officials after they took control around 1899 to get the paths repaired, and the crosses erased once the paths were fixed ("from one end of the island to the other"). William Henry Furness III, *The Island of Stone Money* (1910), [full text](https://archive.org/stream/IslandOfStoneMoney/island%20of%20stone%20money_djvu.txt), retold in Milton Friedman, ["The Island of Stone Money"](https://miltonfriedman.hoover.org/objects/56723/the-island-of-stone-money). 2. Rai were quarried on Palau, more than 400 km (about 250 miles) from Yap, carried by outrigger canoe and raft after months of work; the biggest are about 3.6 m (12 ft) across and weigh about four tons; stones brought with iron tools on foreign ships were valued less than canoe-brought ones; rare in 1840, more than 13,000 on Yap by 1929 — [Bank of Canada Museum, "Rai: big money"](https://www.bankofcanadamuseum.ca/2024/07/rai-big-money/); [Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland, "Island Money" (PDF)](https://www.clevelandfed.org/-/media/project/clevelandfedtenant/clevelandfedsite/publications/economic-commentary/2004/ec-20040201-island-money-pdf.pdf)
3. Rai are still used in traditional exchanges today, such as marriages, inheritance and political deals — [Wikipedia, "Rai stones"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_stones)
4. David O'Keefe, an Irish American sea captain shipwrecked on Yap in the 1870s, brought stones by ship and traded them for copra (dried coconut) — [Wikipedia, "David O'Keefe (ship captain)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_O%27Keefe_(ship_captain))
5. US prices nearly doubled since 2000: the CPI-U annual average was 172.2 in 2000 and a little over 320 in 2025 (about 1.87 times) — [Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis, Consumer Price Index 1913 onward](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/about-us/monetary-policy/inflation-calculator/consumer-price-index-1913-)
6. The phone math is the channel's own: $1,000 ÷ $20 an hour take-home = 50 hours; a full-time work week is 40 hours.
