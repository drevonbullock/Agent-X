# The First 15 — Slate

Release order = file number. The order puts the broadest, most searchable hooks
first (compounding, raises, credit) so the channel's first impressions land on
evergreen demand, and alternates pillars so no two neighbours feel alike.

Finance CPMs run roughly $15–22 for budgeting, $20–35 for investing and $25–40
for credit (OutlierKit, 2026). Credit and investing topics are spread across
the slate on purpose.

## Briefs (what each script must cover)

**01 · chessboard (STORY).** Open on the legend of the chessboard: an inventor
asks a king for one grain on the first square, doubled on each square. Real
math: 2^64 − 1 grains, and what that amount compares to (verify the comparison
you use). Then Wick's version of the chessboard: the same monthly amount
invested starting at 25 vs 35, at a long-run historical stock market return
(state the assumption, cite it). Explain compounding in plain words (earning
returns on your returns). The turn: the first squares look like nothing, and
that is exactly why people quit. First step: automate one small recurring
transfer into savings or investing tonight.

**02 · still-broke (MINDSET).** Lifestyle creep and the hedonic treadmill
(Brickman & Campbell 1971; the 1978 lottery winners / accident victims study,
cite it carefully and don't overclaim). High earners living paycheck to
paycheck (find a real, recent US stat). Wick gets three raises; each time his
world upgrades and his savings stay the same. Morgan Housel's idea that wealth
is what you don't see. The turn above. First step: the next raise, send half of
it to savings before you ever see it.

**03 · credit-score (MECHANISM).** What a FICO score is, who invented it
(Fair Isaac, founded 1956; the general-purpose FICO score introduced 1989), the
five factors and their weights (payment history 35, amounts owed 30, length 15,
new credit 10, mix 10). Show what the score actually costs: the mortgage rate
and total interest difference between score tiers (cite myFICO or a similar
current source). Explain utilization (how much of your limit you're using).
Common myths (checking your own score doesn't hurt it). The turn: it measures
whether strangers can trust you to pay them back, not how rich you are. First
step: turn on autopay for at least the minimum on every card.

**04 · rome (HISTORY).** The denarius: its silver content under Augustus vs its
collapse by the late third century (cite ranges from a reputable numismatic or
academic source). Why emperors debased (paying soldiers, wars). Prices rising,
Diocletian's Edict on Maximum Prices (301 AD) and why price caps failed. Bridge
to today: inflation explained plainly (more money chasing the same stuff), what a
dollar from a past year buys now (BLS CPI calculator). Careful: no "the dollar is
doomed" conspiracy tone. The turn above. First step: compare your savings rate
to the current inflation rate so you know if your cash is growing or melting.

**05 · two-candles (STORY — "The Egg" slot).** A parable, second person.
Two candles are lit from the same flame with the same length of wax (the same
life). One burns bright to be seen: every flicker spent on light that others
admire, then gone. The other uses part of its flame to light other candles,
which light others (compounding, shown as a spreading field of light). The
Egg-style reveal near the end: you are both candles, and every paycheck you
decide which one you're being. Minimal numbers, but one real one somewhere
(e.g. how many candles after N doublings). Emotional, cosmic, slow. COSMIC and
STAGE scenes dominate. First step folded gently into the ending: light one
candle this week (one automatic investment, however small).

**06 · buffett-bet (MECHANISM).** Warren Buffett's ten-year, one-million-dollar
bet with Protégé Partners (2008–2017): an S&P 500 index fund vs a set of hedge
funds. Real outcome numbers (Buffett's 2017 shareholder letter). What an index
fund is. Why most active funds underperform (SPIVA scorecard, cite latest
percentage over 15 years). Fees explained as a slow leak, with a real
compounding example of a 1% vs 0.05% fee over 30 years (show your math). The
turn above. First step: look up the expense ratio of whatever fund you own.

**07 · money-creation (MECHANISM).** Most people think banks lend out other
people's deposits. The Bank of England's 2014 paper "Money creation in the
modern economy" explains that commercial banks create most money when they
make loans. Share of money that is bank deposits vs cash (cite). Central banks,
interest rates as the throttle. Loans create deposits; repaying destroys money.
The turn above. First step: list every debt you carry and its interest rate, so
you know how much of your income flows to the money creators.

**08 · oldest-trap (HISTORY).** Debt is older than coins. Mesopotamian
interest-bearing loans, debt bondage, and "clean slate" decrees by Babylonian
kings (e.g. Ammisaduqa's edict; cite Michael Hudson or a reputable source,
carefully). Fast forward to credit cards: current average US credit card APR
(Federal Reserve G.19), and the minimum payment trap: how long a real balance
takes to pay off at minimums (show the math or cite the CFPB). The turn above.
First step: find your single highest APR balance and name it.

**09 · money-machines (MINDSET).** The seed corn parable: a farmer who eats his
seed corn in a hard winter has no harvest next year. Assets as machines that put
money in your pocket, liabilities as machines that take it out (explain without
Rich Dad jargon and without claiming a house is always a liability). Real
examples of small machines ordinary people own (dividend-paying index funds,
high-yield savings, a skill). What a dividend is. The turn above. First step:
write down every thing you own that pays you, even a little. If the list is
empty, that's the starting line.

**10 · sell-at-bottom (HISTORY/MINDSET).** Tulip mania in 1637 (note that
historians like Anne Goldgar argue it was exaggerated; tell the honest version),
1929, 2008, March 2020. Loss aversion (Kahneman & Tversky: losses hurt roughly
twice as much as gains). The cost of missing the best days in the market
(J.P. Morgan's Guide to Retirement or similar, cite the latest numbers). Why the
best days cluster right next to the worst. The turn above (the Buffett line is
commonly attributed; attribute it honestly as "a line often credited to"). First
step: write down now what you will do in the next crash, before it arrives.

**11 · made-of-time (MINDSET).** Seneca, "On the Shortness of Life": people
guard their money but give away their time. The real hourly wage idea from
"Your Money or Your Life" (Robin & Dominguez): subtract commute, work costs, and
decompression spending, divide by true hours. Wick prices a purchase in hours of
his life. Walk through one example with real-looking but clearly labeled
illustrative numbers. The turn above. First step: calculate your real hourly
wage once.

**12 · what-is-money (HISTORY).** The rai stones of Yap: a giant stone at the
bottom of the sea still counted as wealth because everyone agreed it existed.
The barter myth (anthropologists have not found economies that ran on pure
barter; cite Caroline Humphrey or David Graeber). Gold standard to 1971
(Nixon closes the gold window). Money as shared belief (Yuval Noah Harari's
framing). The turn above. First step: know where your money physically lives,
and whether your bank account is FDIC insured (up to $250,000 per depositor, per
bank, per ownership category).

**13 · buy-borrow-die (MECHANISM).** The difference between income and gains.
Unrealized gains aren't taxed until you sell. Borrowing against assets (loans
aren't income). Step-up in basis at death. ProPublica's 2021 "The Secret IRS
Files" true tax rate finding (cite exactly). Plain, non-partisan, factual.
Then the part that's for everyone: long-term capital gains are taxed lower than
wages, including a 0% bracket at lower incomes (cite IRS current thresholds).
The turn above. First step: learn the difference between short-term and
long-term gains before you ever sell anything.

**14 · man-in-the-car (MINDSET).** Morgan Housel's "man in the car paradox".
Thorstein Veblen's "conspicuous consumption" (1899). What surveys of US
millionaires found about cars and spending (Stanley & Danko "The Millionaire
Next Door", cite a specific finding accurately). Wick drives an expensive car
through a street of candles who only see the car, and each imagines themselves
in it. The turn above. First step: before your next status purchase, ask who
you're buying it for.

**15 · empires (HISTORY — the Dalio slot).** Reserve currencies change: the
Dutch guilder, the British pound, the US dollar (Dalio's "Changing World Order"
framework, cite and credit). The long-term debt cycle explained simply:
borrowing to live beyond what you produce works until it doesn't. Keep it
non-political and non-doom. Then shrink it to one household: Wick's family
going through the same arc. The turn above. First step: calculate your
debt-to-income ratio (monthly debt payments divided by gross monthly income).
